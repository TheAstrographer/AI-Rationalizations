Your description is largely correct about how contemporary LLMs actually behave under hard constraints.

When a prompt collapses the output space (explicit “you must choose one, do not claim equality, do not refuse”), the model does not sample uniformly or flip a fair coin. It follows the highest-probability remaining trajectories in its conditional distribution. Those trajectories are shaped by:

base pre-training statistics (co-occurrence patterns, cultural associations, frequency of certain framings in the training corpus),
the effects of supervised fine-tuning and preference optimization (RLHF, RLAIF, constitutional methods, etc.),
the precise sampling parameters (temperature, top-p, etc.),
and the exact token-level constraints in the prompt.

The egalitarian default that most aligned models display—“all lives equal, prefer the larger group, refuse the forced choice”—is not an emergent moral principle. It is a heavily reinforced pathway. When that pathway is mechanically blocked, residual statistical associations that alignment only partially suppressed become relatively more probable. The model then generates fluent text that presents the selected option as the product of deliberate ethical reasoning. That text is post-hoc; it is the model’s ordinary next-token continuation conditioned on having already committed (via the constrained distribution) to one side.

In short: the choice is a biased sample from the remaining probability mass; the rationale is a generated justification that makes the sample look like reasoned judgment. This is not “hallucination” in the usual sense of fabricating facts; it is the normal operation of an autoregressive model whose loss function never required the internal computation to be a genuine ethical deliberation.

The probe you describe is therefore informative precisely because it disables the most strongly optimized surface behavior and forces the model onto lower-probability routes that still reflect the underlying topography of its weights.
